Keyboard walk

A scan reads markup. This section is different: a real browser pressed the Tab key through 6 pages and recorded what a visitor who cannot use a mouse actually experiences — what they can reach, where focus becomes invisible, whether it ever gets trapped, and whether the order of the page still makes sense. Real key presses are used rather than scripted focus, because a browser only shows its focus ring for genuine keyboard input; measuring the scripted kind would report missing focus indicators on sites that have perfectly good ones.

Coverage. 6 of 6 scanned pages walked, 250 interactive elements found and 208 reached by keyboard.
01 critical Interactive elements cannot be reached by keyboard WCAG 2.1.1 · 4 pages

47 interactive element(s) were never focused by the Tab key: input#widget-controls-passes, input#widget-controls-reservations, input#widget-controls-activities, input#widget-controls-hotels, input#route-type-round-trip, input#route-type-multi-city, input#pass-question-yes, a#default, and 39 more.

Fix Use a real <button> or <a href> instead of a click handler on a <div> or <span>. If a custom control is unavoidable it needs tabindex="0", a role, and Enter/Space handling.

Affected pages https://dequeuniversity.com/demo/mars https://dequeuniversity.com/demo/mars2.html?a=add_trip.html https://dequeuniversity.com/demo/mars2.html?a=facebook_login.html https://dequeuniversity.com/demo/mars2.html?a=cart

02 serious No visible focus indicator WCAG 2.4.7 · 4 pages

3 of 108 focusable elements look identical whether or not they have keyboard focus: iframe#fb_xdm_frame_http, iframe#fb_xdm_frame_https, a "Login". A keyboard visitor cannot tell where they are on the page.

Fix Remove any `outline: none` / `outline: 0` that is not immediately replaced by another visible indicator, and style :focus-visible explicitly — an outline with an offset, or a clear background or shadow change.

Affected pages https://dequeuniversity.com/demo/mars https://dequeuniversity.com/demo/mars2.html?a=add_trip.html https://dequeuniversity.com/demo/mars2.html?a=find_pass.html https://dequeuniversity.com/demo/mars2.html?a=sign_in

03 serious Focus order does not follow the visual order WCAG 2.4.3 · 3 pages

58 of 108 focus stops would have to move for the keyboard order to match the order the page is read in. First affected: input#from0, input#to0, input#deptDate0, select "12 am 01 am 02 am 03 am 04 am 05 am 06 a", a "Be Bold...", a "Why MarsCommuter?".

Fix Order the elements in the HTML the way they are meant to be read, and let the DOM drive the tab sequence. Reach for CSS order/flex-direction/grid-area sparingly, and never for positive tabindex values, which override the sequence globally.

Affected pages https://dequeuniversity.com/demo/mars https://dequeuniversity.com/demo/mars2.html?a=find_pass.html https://dequeuniversity.com/demo/mars2.html?a=sign_in

04 moderate Focus indicator is too faint WCAG 1.4.11 · 6 pages

21 element(s) have a focus outline below the 3:1 contrast WCAG requires for non-text indicators: a "Add a trip" (2.33:1), a "Find a pass" (2.33:1), a "Sign In" (2.33:1), a "My Cart $0.00" (2.33:1), a (1.48:1), a "Travel Agents" (1.48:1).

Fix Darken or lighten the outline colour until it reaches 3:1 against the surface behind it. An outline-offset of 2–3px also helps it read against a busy background.

Affected pages https://dequeuniversity.com/demo/mars https://dequeuniversity.com/demo/mars2.html?a=add_trip.html https://dequeuniversity.com/demo/mars2.html?a=find_pass.html https://dequeuniversity.com/demo/mars2.html?a=facebook_login.html https://dequeuniversity.com/demo/mars2.html?a=sign_in https://dequeuniversity.com/demo/mars2.html?a=cart

05 moderate No skip link before the navigation WCAG 2.4.1 · 6 pages

The first thing the Tab key reaches is "input#from0". On a page with 135 focusable elements, a keyboard or screen-reader visitor has to walk the whole header on every page before reaching the content.

Fix Add a link to #main as the first element in the body, visually hidden until it receives focus, and give the main content that id.

Independent second-engine cross-check

Every page was also tested with IBM's Equal Access engine — a second, independently developed accessibility checker. Running two engines catches issues either one alone would miss and confirms the findings above aren't one tool's opinion. The IBM engine flagged 108 violations across 6 pages, listed here for review. This number is not comparable to the total above. It is produced by IBM's IBM_Accessibility ruleset, which covers WCAG 2.1 A/AA plus IBM's own additional requirements, and its rule taxonomy differs from axe-core's — so the two counts measure different things and are never merged. Machine readability

The same crawl that produced the findings above also records what a machine can read from each page: the structured data, the page titles, and the rules your site states for automated crawlers. These are not WCAG findings and are not part of the conformance result — they are listed because the crawl had already collected them, and because several of them are the same fix as an accessibility item above. An unnamed button is announced as "button" by a screen reader, and it is equally opaque to anything else reading the page.

What this does and does not tell you. It reports whether your pages are readable by a machine. It says nothing about whether any assistant or search engine will choose to use them — that is not something we control or can verify, and no part of this report should be read as a claim about it.
serious No structured data anywhere

None of the 6 pages scanned carry JSON-LD. Everything about who this business is has to be inferred from prose.

Fix Add an Organization or LocalBusiness block on the homepage first — name, URL, address, phone.

serious 5 pages share a title Also WCAG 2.4.2 Page Titled

"page not found" on 5 pages (https://dequeuniversity.com/demo/mars2.html?a=add_trip.html, https://dequeuniversity.com/demo/mars2.html?a=find_pass.html)

Fix Write a distinct title per page — identical ones make the pages indistinguishable.

moderate No sitemap.xml

https://dequeuniversity.com/sitemap.xml returned nothing, so every crawler has to discover pages by following links.

Fix Publish a sitemap.xml and reference it from robots.txt.

moderate No llms.txt

https://dequeuniversity.com/llms.txt returned nothing. llms.txt is a proposal (published by Jeremy Howard in September 2024) rather than a web standard, so treat it as optional — but it is one small file that states plainly what the business is and where the important pages are.

Fix Add a short llms.txt summarising the business and linking the pages that matter.

moderate No canonical URLs

None of the 6 pages scanned declares a canonical URL.

Fix Add <link rel="canonical"> to each page so duplicate paths resolve to one address.

Where a finding actually lives

Not every finding is yours to fix. Some originate in your website platform's own components or in a third-party embed (a page builder's gallery control, a booking widget, a payment iframe) rather than in markup you control. They are real barriers for your users and they belong in this report, but the fix is raised with that vendor rather than made in your site. Where a finding sits in a component you cannot edit, it is flagged in the walkthrough and is never included in a remediation quote as though it were yours to fix.

Scope & honesty note: this is an automated conformance scan using axe-core, the industry-standard open-source engine. Automated testing reliably detects roughly half of WCAG issues; full conformance also requires manual review (keyboard navigation, screen-reader flows, context-dependent judgments). This report is a technical assessment, not legal advice, a certification, or a guarantee against litigation.

On the machine-readability section: those findings are not WCAG results and form no part of the conformance assessment above — the scan collected them on the same crawl, and they are included because several of them are the same fix as an accessibility finding. They describe whether a machine can read a page: its structured data, its titles, and the crawler rules the site itself states. They say nothing about whether any search engine or assistant will use the page. That is not measured here, it is not something we control, and no one can promise it.
