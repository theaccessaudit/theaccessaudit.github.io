Keyboard walk

A scan reads markup. This section is different: a real browser pressed the Tab key through 4 pages and recorded what a visitor who cannot use a mouse actually experiences — what they can reach, where focus becomes invisible, whether it ever gets trapped, and whether the order of the page still makes sense. Real key presses are used rather than scripted focus, because a browser only shows its focus ring for genuine keyboard input; measuring the scripted kind would report missing focus indicators on sites that have perfectly good ones.

Coverage. 4 of 4 scanned pages walked, 62 interactive elements found and 58 reached by keyboard.
01 critical Interactive elements cannot be reached by keyboard WCAG 2.1.1 · 3 pages

4 interactive element(s) were never focused by the Tab key: div "S", div "M", div "L", div "Add to cart".

Fix Use a real <button> or <a href> instead of a click handler on a <div> or <span>. If a custom control is unavoidable it needs tabindex="0", a role, and Enter/Space handling.

Affected pages https://theaccessaudit.github.io/demo-store/mug.html https://theaccessaudit.github.io/demo-store/bowl.html https://theaccessaudit.github.io/demo-store/cart.html

02 serious No visible focus indicator WCAG 2.4.7 · 4 pages

17 of 17 focusable elements look identical whether or not they have keyboard focus: a "The Access Audit", a "Northwind Ceramics", a "Shop", a "Mugs", a "Bowls", a "Cart", button, button, and 9 more. A keyboard visitor cannot tell where they are on the page.

Fix Remove any `outline: none` / `outline: 0` that is not immediately replaced by another visible indicator, and style :focus-visible explicitly — an outline with an offset, or a clear background or shadow change.

Affected pages https://theaccessaudit.github.io/demo-store/ https://theaccessaudit.github.io/demo-store/mug.html https://theaccessaudit.github.io/demo-store/bowl.html https://theaccessaudit.github.io/demo-store/cart.html

03 serious Focus order does not follow the visual order WCAG 2.4.3 · 1 page

4 of 15 focus stops would have to move for the keyboard order to match the order the page is read in. First affected: a "The Access Audit", a "Northwind Ceramics", a "Shop", a "Mugs".

Fix Order the elements in the HTML the way they are meant to be read, and let the DOM drive the tab sequence. Reach for CSS order/flex-direction/grid-area sparingly, and never for positive tabindex values, which override the sequence globally.

Affected pages https://theaccessaudit.github.io/demo-store/mug.html

04 moderate No skip link before the navigation WCAG 2.4.1 · 4 pages

The first thing the Tab key reaches is "a "The Access Audit"". On a page with 17 focusable elements, a keyboard or screen-reader visitor has to walk the whole header on every page before reaching the content.

Fix Add a link to #main as the first element in the body, visually hidden until it receives focus, and give the main content that id.

Affected pages https://theaccessaudit.github.io/demo-store/ https://theaccessaudit.github.io/demo-store/mug.html https://theaccessaudit.github.io/demo-store/bowl.html https://theaccessaudit.github.io/demo-store/cart.html

Independent second-engine cross-check

Every page was also tested with IBM's Equal Access engine — a second, independently developed accessibility checker. Running two engines catches issues either one alone would miss and confirms the findings above aren't one tool's opinion. The IBM engine flagged 50 violations across 4 pages, listed here for review. This number is not comparable to the total above. It is produced by IBM's IBM_Accessibility ruleset, which covers WCAG 2.1 A/AA plus IBM's own additional requirements, and its rule taxonomy differs from axe-core's — so the two counts measure different things and are never merged. Machine readability

The same crawl that produced the findings above also records what a machine can read from each page: the structured data, the page titles, and the rules your site states for automated crawlers. These are not WCAG findings and are not part of the conformance result — they are listed because the crawl had already collected them, and because several of them are the same fix as an accessibility item above. An unnamed button is announced as "button" by a screen reader, and it is equally opaque to anything else reading the page.

What this does and does not tell you. It reports whether your pages are readable by a machine. It says nothing about whether any assistant or search engine will choose to use them — that is not something we control or can verify, and no part of this report should be read as a claim about it.
serious No structured data anywhere

None of the 4 pages scanned carry JSON-LD. Everything about who this business is has to be inferred from prose.

Fix Add an Organization or LocalBusiness block on the homepage first — name, URL, address, phone.

serious 2 pages share a title Also WCAG 2.4.2 Page Titled

"products — northwind ceramics" on 2 pages (https://theaccessaudit.github.io/demo-store/mug.html, https://theaccessaudit.github.io/demo-store/bowl.html)

Fix Write a distinct title per page — identical ones make the pages indistinguishable.

moderate No canonical URLs

None of the 4 pages scanned declares a canonical URL.

Fix Add <link rel="canonical"> to each page so duplicate paths resolve to one address.

moderate 3 pages share a meta description

"small-batch stoneware, thrown and glazed by hand in ojai, ca" on 3 pages (https://theaccessaudit.github.io/demo-store/, https://theaccessaudit.github.io/demo-store/mug.html)

Fix Write a distinct meta description per page — identical ones make the pages indistinguishable.

Where a finding actually lives

Not every finding is yours to fix. Some originate in your website platform's own components or in a third-party embed (a page builder's gallery control, a booking widget, a payment iframe) rather than in markup you control. They are real barriers for your users and they belong in this report, but the fix is raised with that vendor rather than made in your site. Where a finding sits in a component you cannot edit, it is flagged in the walkthrough and is never included in a remediation quote as though it were yours to fix.

Scope & honesty note: this is an automated conformance scan using axe-core, the industry-standard open-source engine. This report is a technical assessment, not legal advice, a certification, or a guarantee against litigation.
